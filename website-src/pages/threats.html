Threats to Validity (§21)

Every claim in this report is subject to threats of one of three kinds: internal (does the causal chain from tests to numbers hold within this study?), external (would our conclusions generalise?), and construct (do our metrics actually measure what we claim they measure?). Full text in docs/threats-to-validity.md.

Internal validity

I-1. Weak oracle in the original PICT-driven tests

PictRegexTest's two oracles (smoke + double-negation on one input) are broad but shallow. This was the confounder in our early CIT experiment. We addressed it by adding a second oracle in PictMetamorphicTest and showing both sets of numbers explicitly (see CIT page).

I-2. PIT non-determinism from timeouts

PIT reports 1–14 TIMED_OUT mutants per run. Which specific mutants time out shifts run-to-run. Total noise is bounded at ~1 percentage point on the aggregate mutation score.

I-3. PIT filter-flag bug caught late

An earlier version of the per-strength CIT experiment used -Dgroups=<tag> (Surefire's filter) to isolate interaction strengths. PIT uses its own JUnit engine and silently ignores the Surefire flag — so every "per-strength" PIT run was actually running the full 1948-test suite. The apparent "flat" mutation scores we initially reported (~50.7% across all strengths) were the full-suite score with PIT run-to-run noise. Fixed by switching to -DincludedGroups=<tag> (PIT's own filter). All CIT numbers on this site are from the corrected runs; see the AI Tools page for the correction story.

I-4. Datatypes.buildAll unreachable

BRICS' Datatypes.buildAll reads a companion resource src/Unicode.txt not shipped in our vendored source. Result: 180 of 187 mutations in that class are NO_COVERAGE, pulling the aggregate mutation score down by ~9 percentage points. Fixable by vendoring the file, but doing so would count as a "modification to BRICS" that changes the surface being evaluated.

External validity

E-1. Single-commit case study

All findings apply to BRICS commit 582d8f3. The three bugs we identified may not exist in earlier or later commits. Commit hash pinned in BRICS_COMMIT.

E-2. Scope of quantitative claims

All coverage / mutation numbers are scoped to dk.brics.automaton.*. We don't claim these conclusions transfer to other regex libraries.

E-3. Development environment (Windows + Temurin)

All development happened on Windows 11 + Temurin JDK 21.0.8.9. PICT is a Windows binary. Mitigated by GitHub Actions CI running the JUnit + PIT pipeline on Ubuntu-latest and verifying the same numbers reproduce.

E-4. AI-authored tests may share systematic blind spots

Most of the test suite was AI-authored (see AI Tools). If the AI has a systematic weakness (always ASCII, always short inputs, forgets Unicode edge cases), that bias is baked into the whole suite. Mitigated by human review at each session and by the PIT filter-bug correction — which specifically shows a case where human review caught something the AI missed.

Construct validity

C-1. Line coverage overstates suite strength

Our 77% line coverage vs 51% mutation score is itself evidence of this: high coverage doesn't guarantee a suite that catches bugs. We report both metrics side-by-side rather than relying on line coverage alone.

C-2. Mutation score is not equivalence-adjusted

PIT reports raw kill percentage. Some fraction of surviving mutants are equivalent (semantically identical to the original, unkillable). Our 5-mutant analysis rates one of the five as equivalent under our oracle. Rate probably similar (~20%) across the population, which would put the effective mutation score closer to ~62%.

C-3. Kill rate is a proxy, not a direct fault-detection measure

Killing a mutant means a test noticed a change, not that the test would notice a real bug. We mitigate by reporting a diverse mix of testing techniques (example-based, property-based, differential, mutation) — different flavours catch different fault classes. The Datatypes.exists NPE we found came from coverage-guided test writing, not mutation.

C-4. PICT model design bias

Our 7 parameters and 4 constraints reflect our judgment about which BRICS input dimensions matter. Surrogate pairs, control characters, extremely deep nesting are outside the model. Model is versioned and each parameter has a "values NOT chosen" section in docs/pict-rationale.md.

What would strengthen this study

  1. Vendor src/Unicode.txt and re-run PIT with Datatypes.buildAll reachable — likely lifts mutation score by ~5–8 points.
  2. Second independent reviewer rates each of the ~450 surviving mutants for equivalence, producing a corrected score.
  3. Run the same suite against a second BRICS commit for consistency.
  4. Extend the PICT model with Unicode surrogate-pair alphabets.
